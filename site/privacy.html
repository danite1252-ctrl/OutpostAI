The short version

Outpost runs on a computer you own. Your chats, pictures, recordings and everything else you do in it are stored there, not with us. We don't run any servers for Outpost, we don't have accounts for you, and the apps contain no ads, analytics or tracking.

What this covers

This policy covers Outpost for Windows and Linux, the Outpost app for Android, and this website. "Outpost" here means the software. "Your Outpost" means the computer you install it on, which the rest of your devices connect to. "We" means the people who make Outpost.

Your Outpost is run by you, or by whoever set it up in your household (its owner). The owner, not us, decides who can use it and controls the information on it.

What we collect

Nothing. Outpost doesn't send us your conversations, files, recordings, usage statistics, crash reports, device identifiers or contact details. It has no advertising and doesn't share or sell information to anyone. Logs and crash reports are kept on your Outpost for you to look at and are never sent anywhere.

If you contact us (for example by reporting a problem on GitHub), we'll see what you choose to send, and use it only to reply and fix the problem.

What stays on your Outpost

These are stored on your Outpost's own disk:

  • Chats, personas, and what personas remember about you
  • Documents and folders you add to chats
  • Pictures, videos and 3D models you make or upload, and the gallery
  • Voice recordings while they're being turned into text
  • Characters trained from your own photos
  • Accounts, PINs (stored only in a protected, scrambled form), paired devices and settings
  • Logs and crash reports

The AI models run on your Outpost (or on another computer in your home that you've chosen to lend its graphics card), so what you type, say and upload is processed there too.

When Outpost uses the internet

Outpost works without the internet once it's set up. It goes online only for the following, and never sends your chats, files or recordings as part of them:

WhenWhat happens
Checking for updatesOutpost and the Android app ask GitHub for the latest version, and download it when you choose to update (or automatically, if you've turned that on). You can switch update checks off.
Downloading modelsWhen you download an AI model or one of the components Outpost needs, it comes from the site that publishes it, such as Hugging Face or GitHub.
Tools you addIf you give a chat a tool that uses an online service, such as web search, a Google account or a smart home system, the chat sends that service what's needed for the request (a search query, for example). Those services handle it under their own privacy policies. Outpost has no such tools turned on unless you add them.
Notifications you set upIf you tell Outpost to notify another app or address about events, it sends the events you chose there.
Using it away from homeIf you set up remote access (for example with a private network service like Tailscale), your devices reach your Outpost through it, encrypted end to end.

Like any website, GitHub, Hugging Face and the other sites above can see your internet address when Outpost downloads from them.

The Android app

The Android app connects only to the Outpost it's paired with. Pairing uses a QR code or a one-time code, and the app then checks that it's really your Outpost before every connection, so it can't be sent somewhere else. Everything between them is encrypted.

The app asks for these permissions, each only when you use the feature:

PermissionWhy
CameraTo scan the pairing QR code. Nothing from the camera is saved or sent.
MicrophoneFor voice: what you say goes to your Outpost to be understood and answered. The Mic button may use your phone's built-in speech recognition when it can, which works under your phone maker's or Google's privacy terms. Hands-free conversations always use your Outpost.
Photos and filesOnly the pictures or files you pick to send, and saving things you choose to your phone.
NotificationsTo tell you when something you started, like a video or a 3D model, is ready.
Running in the backgroundTo keep checking on a long job while the app is closed, so the notification arrives on time.
Wi-Fi and the local networkTo find your Outpost on your home Wi-Fi and connect to it.
Fingerprint or face unlockOptional, to unlock the app. Your fingerprint or face never leaves your phone; Android only tells the app whether it matched.
Installing updatesTo install a newer version of the app that it downloaded from GitHub. You can switch this off.

The app keeps the pairing details for your Outpost on your phone, protected by Android. It doesn't read your contacts, location, messages or other apps.

Accounts and children

Accounts exist only on your Outpost. The owner creates them by inviting people, and each person picks their own PIN. Each person's chats and pictures are their own; owners manage accounts but Outpost doesn't show them other people's chats.

Owners can make restricted accounts for children, which can only use what the owner allows. We don't collect information from anyone, including children. Parents and guardians who run an Outpost are responsible for how their children use it.

Seeing and deleting your data

Because everything is on your Outpost, you and your household's owner are in control of it:

  • Chats, pictures and documents: delete them in the app at any time. Deleting a chat also deletes what was learned from it.
  • Your account: an owner can delete any account, which deletes that person's chats, pictures and paired devices with it.
  • A phone: unpair it from the app; it's signed out at once. Uninstalling the Android app removes everything it stored on the phone.
  • Everything: uninstall Outpost and delete its data folder on the computer it runs on.

We hold no data about you, so there's nothing for us to delete or hand over. If you'd like help finding something on your own Outpost, contact us below.

Security

Outpost encrypts the connection between your devices and your Outpost, protects accounts with PINs, and asks for the PIN again before sensitive changes. Commands that a chat wants to run on a computer need your approval first. Because your Outpost runs on your own hardware, keeping that computer up to date and physically secure is up to you.

This website

This website doesn't use cookies, analytics, ads or anything loaded from other companies; even its fonts come from this site. The service that hosts it may keep standard, short-lived access logs (such as internet addresses and the pages requested) to keep the site running and protect it from abuse.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the date at the top. Changes that affect what Outpost does with your information will also be mentioned in the release notes for that version.

Contact

Questions about privacy or this policy: open an issue on Outpost's GitHub page.
